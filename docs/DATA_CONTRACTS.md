# Data contracts

The site consumes only `site/public/data/*.json`, validated against `data/contracts/<name>.schema.json`
(JSON Schema 2020-12, generated by `pipelines/contracts.py`, the single source of truth).
Every artifact: `schema_version: "v1"`, a `tags` map (field group to honesty tag), compact sorted JSON, no NaN (missing is `null`).
Tags: OBSERVED / DERIVED / PREDICTED / SIMULATED (SYNTHETIC unused) and "/" combinations.

## Conventions
- Money is GBP as recorded. Line amount = Quantity x Price. Weeks start Monday (ISO). Time is the source's naive local time.
- Customers appear only as an anonymised integer `key` (dense order of first purchase). Source Customer IDs are never published.
- Columnar arrays (`customers.*`) have equal length. `null` means not observed.
- Size budget: each artifact under 400 KB gzip; measured sizes go to `meta.json.artifact_bytes`.

## Line classes (every kept row has exactly one)
Precedence: nonproduct stock code first, then credit-note prefix, then zero/negative quantity or price, otherwise sale.
| class | rule | in revenue totals | in product analytics | in customer analytics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sale | numeric invoice, product stock code, Quantity > 0, Price > 0 | yes | yes | yes if Customer ID present |
| refund | invoice starts with `C` (credit note), product stock code | yes (negative) | refund counts only | yes (reduces monetary, not frequency) |
| stock_adjustment | no `C` prefix, Quantity <= 0 with Price = 0 (write-offs, "damaged", "lost"), or positive Quantity with Price = 0 | amount is 0 | no | no |
| nonproduct | stock code is not a product code (postage, manual, discount, fees, vouchers, test, bad debt) | yes | no | no |

Product stock code = 5 digits plus up to 3 letters (upper-cased), or `DCGS` plus 4 digits and an optional letter.
`nonproduct` subclasses: postage (POST, DOT, C2, C3, CARRIAGE codes), manual_adjustment (M, ADJUST*, D discount, S samples, B bad debt),
fees (BANK CHARGES, AMAZONFEE, CRUK), voucher (gift_*), test (TEST*), other.
Stock codes are upper-cased and trimmed to form the SKU key (e.g. `85123a` and `85123A` are one SKU).

## Row exclusions (the only rows removed from the star schema)
1. `overlap_duplicate`: rows from sheet `Year 2009-2010` dated on or after 2010-12-01 (identical copies exist in sheet 2).
2. `exact_duplicate`: after (1), rows identical on all eight source columns to an earlier row. Rule stated, count published,
   GBP effect published as a sensitivity (some may be genuine repeated scans).

## Customer scope
Rows with no Customer ID ("guest") stay in revenue totals and are excluded from RFM, cohorts, CLV; their share is published.
Baskets use all invoices with product sale lines (guest included); the basket key is the invoice.

## Refund treatment
Credit-note lines (`C` invoices) are refunds. Each is matched, within the same Customer ID and SKU, to the most recent earlier
sale lines with unrefunded quantity (LIFO). Matched, unmatched-no-customer, unmatched-no-prior-sale and over-refund quantities are all published.
Refunds are never netted into the sale lines they match; they reduce monetary value and are excluded from frequency/purchase counts.

## Elasticity decision rule
**Stage 1 (price variation, fixed before the evidence was computed).** Weekly price of a SKU = the most frequent unit price among its sale lines
that week (ties: higher). A SKU qualifies when it has at least 26 weeks with sales, at most 90% of those weeks at its overall modal price, and at
least 3 price regimes (distinct weekly prices each observed in at least 4 weeks). Stage 1 passes if at least 30 SKUs qualify.

**Stage 2 (identification), added after Stage 1 was computed.** Stage 1 passed by a wide margin (see `elasticity.json`), but the price spread
looked like customer-level price tiers rather than list-price changes, so a second test was written before any elasticity regression was run.
Using the qualifying SKUs only: (a) the share of within-SKU log-price variance that lies *within* a single SKU-week must be at most 25%
(otherwise price differs by buyer, not by time); (b) for SKU-weeks with several prices and at least 5 lines, the median Spearman correlation between
line quantity and unit price must be above -0.2 in absolute value (otherwise price is set by order size, which makes price endogenous to quantity).
Elasticity is **estimable** only if both stages pass. Otherwise the studio declares "not estimable", publishes the price-variation evidence,
and says what would be needed. No elasticity number is ever shown in that case. The stage-2 amendment is disclosed in the artifact and in `LIMITATIONS.md`.

## Backtest and CLV design (fixed up front)
- Forecast: weekly units, complete Monday weeks only. Rolling origin, expanding window, minimum 60 training weeks, horizons 1 to 8 weeks evaluated (pooled tables at 1, 2, 4, 8), forecast out to 8.
  Baseline = seasonal-naive with lag 52. Metrics: MAE, WAPE, MASE, bias, skill = 1 - MAE_model / MAE_snaive. Models that lose are published.
- CLV: calibration 2009-12-01 to 2011-06-09, holdout 2011-06-10 to 2011-12-09 (26 weeks). Transactions are distinct purchase days per customer.
  Compared against a no-model baseline (every customer gets the average calibration repeat rate scaled by exposure).
- Newsvendor: SIMULATED. Cost, salvage and review-period assumptions are an explicit table. Python reference vectors with tolerance 1e-6 relative.

## Data requests from the creative direction (P09-DATA-002, DR-1 to DR-10)
All ten are supportable from the ledger. Each is computed in Python from the cleaned star schema; none copies a number from the design document.
| DR | Contract | Rule or definition |
|---|---|---|
| DR-1 | `facts.hero_pair_{a,b,a_desc,b_desc,na,nb,nab,chance,lift}`, `facts.hero_selection_rule`, `baskets.hero` | Over all pairs of product SKUs (not limited to the 0.5% support rule), the pair with the most baskets in common; ties: higher lift, then SKU order. The anchor `a` is the larger set. `chance = na * nb / basket_count`, `lift = nab * basket_count / (na * nb)`. Runner-up pairs are published. The hero edge is also in `baskets.edges` with `hero: true`. |
| DR-2 | `baskets.showcase`, `baskets.rules[].variant_of_same_product` | Six rule indices into `baskets.rules`: the 3 highest-lift rules then the 3 highest-count rules, all with `variant_of_same_product` false, the hero pair excluded, no itemset twice (a rule and its reverse count once). Selection runs over all mined rules, and the six are forced into the published list. |
| DR-3 | `cohorts.active_customers`, `active_revenue_gbp`, `active_net_revenue_gbp` | Aligned to `cohort_months`. Known customers with at least one sale line in the calendar month (the cohort activity definition). `active_revenue_gbp` = their sale-line GBP (the basis of `revenue_retention`); `active_net_revenue_gbp` also nets their credit-note lines. `overview.series.active_customers` uses the same definition and also covers the partial month 2011-12 (tested for equality on shared months). |
| DR-4 | `rfm.grid.{r_by_m,f_by_m}_{customers,revenue_gbp}` | 5 x 5, row = first axis score 1 to 5, column = second axis score; same scoring as `r_by_f`; `grid.axes` states the orientation. |
| DR-5 | `backtest.pooled[].{actual_total, forecast_total, over_total, under_total}`, `backtest.design.error_sign` | Sums over all series and origins at that scope and horizon. Error `e = forecast - actual`, so positive bias means over-forecast. `over_total = sum max(f - a, 0)`, `under_total = sum max(a - f, 0)`. Identities: `over + under = sum |e| = MAE * n`; `actual - under = sum min(a, f)`; `wape = (over + under) / actual`. Pooled rows exist for horizons 1, 2, 4, 8 only. |
| DR-6 | `clv.holdout.{overlap_customer_sum, baseline_predicted_total, baseline_overlap_customer_sum}`, `clv.deciles[].{baseline_predicted, overlap_sum, predicted_sum, actual_sum, baseline_predicted_sum}` | Customer level sums of min(predicted_i, actual_i); baseline = population-average repeat rate times holdout weeks for every customer. |
| DR-7 | `newsvendor_scenarios.scenarios[].expected_{sales,leftover,shortage}_{normal,empirical}`, `newsvendor_vectors` | Every item at the default assumptions for both demand models is a parity vector (vectors now carry `item`); scenario rows at the defaults equal those vectors exactly (price rounded to 4 decimals, cost and salvage to 6, in both). |
| DR-8 | `reconciliation.refunds.*` | Refunded quantity and value split into matched, `unmatched_no_prior_sale` (customer never bought that SKU before the credit note), `over_refund` (earlier sales existed but were already fully refunded) and `unmatched_no_customer`. Values use each credit-note line's own unit value; the four parts sum to the refunded quantity and value (tested). `sale_units` is the units of sale lines. |
| DR-9 | `elasticity.estimates` | `null` when `decision.estimable` is false (enforced by `if/then` in the schema, so a number cannot be published in that state); otherwise `{method, skus[{sku, description, elasticity, ci_lo, ci_hi, weeks, regimes}], pooled{elasticity, ci_lo, ci_hi}}`. The evidence table is always present. Currently not estimable. |
| DR-10 | `facts.rfm_rf_share_customers`, `rfm_rf_share_revenue`, `backtest_best_model_label` | Share of RFM customers (and of their net GBP) with R score >= 4 and F score >= 4; plain-language label of the best model, also on every `backtest.models[]`. |
